# Adaptive Marathon Plan and Measurement Framework — v3 (16 weeks)

A 16-week, decision-led system for progressing duration, preserving consistency, and adapting to real-world response.

**Core idea.** This is not a rigid mileage prescription. It is a control system: apply enough stress to drive adaptation, then use objective and subjective response data to progress, hold, reduce, substitute, or seek clinical review.

**Event.** BYD Singapore Marathon, race day 4 December 2026. Week 1 starts Monday 17 August 2026; race week is Week 16 (30 Nov – 6 Dec).

| Plan parameter | Working assumption |
|---|---|
| Starting envelope | ~35 km/week with a long run near 15 km, already tolerated. |
| Peak envelope | ~52–55 km/week; peak long run 27–28 km (hard cap: 28 km or 3:15–3:30, whichever comes first). |
| Primary objective | Arrive healthy enough to start and complete the marathon with sustainable pacing and fueling. |
| Priority order | Safety and consistency → duration tolerance → aerobic efficiency → specific durability → taper freshness. |
| Personalization | Heart-rate zones, race pace, medical restrictions, and issue thresholds must be individualized. |

Status: detailed working framework for runner, coach, doctor, physiotherapist, or podiatrist review. It is not a diagnosis, medical clearance, or substitute for individualized clinical advice.

## What changed in v3 (and why)

v3 incorporates an exercise-physiology review and a coaching review of v2. The philosophy is unchanged; the corrections are:

1. **Long run rebalanced against weekly volume.** v2 grew the long run to 30–32 km inside a 46–48 km week (63–67% of the week in one session — the classic recreational-marathoner injury signature). v3 caps the long run at **27–28 km or 3:15–3:30 on feet, whichever comes first**, and adds a **midweek medium-long run (10–14 km) from Week 6**, lifting peak weeks to 52–55 km and holding long-run share ≤ ~55%. Cumulative weekly time-on-feet builds marathon durability more safely than one maximal run; aerobic return beyond ~3 h is marginal relative to bone/tendon cost at this volume.
2. **Quality is a green-week default, not "optional."** "Optional" plus a conservative controller equals "never." Strides, steady blocks, and one short threshold/steady touch are now the default when status is green, and the first thing removed when amber (the amber system *is* the flexibility — no double hedging).
3. **Marathon-pace calibration added.** Progressive MP blocks inside long runs (Week 11: 2×3 km; Week 13: 4–6 km continuous inside the dress rehearsal; Week 14: short MP blocks; Week 15: 3×2 km taper touches). Pacing errors in the opening 10 km are the top self-reported cause of blow-ups, and GI tolerance is intensity-dependent — neither can be rehearsed at easy effort.
4. **Half-marathon tune-up in Week 10** at controlled effort, replacing that week's long run. It provides the pacing evidence the C-goal requires, race-morning logistics rehearsal, fueling under race stress, and a confidence deposit.
5. **Quantified adaptation triggers.** Wearable ambers now have thresholds and require symptom corroboration (§9). Wearable-only signals without symptoms default to *green-easy*, not reduction. The long-run abort rule uses a decoupling number instead of "drifts unexpectedly."
6. **Anti-under-loading guards.** The controller was asymmetrically conservative: many paths to reduce, one vague path to progress. v3 defines a **minimum viable peak** (at least one fueled ≥26 km long run completed by end of Week 13) and replaces Week 13's "skip if *any* gate not green" with binary, checkable gates.
7. **Amber split into life-amber and tissue-amber** with a single action each, plus a circuit-breaker (3 ambers in 7 days, or 2 consecutive reduced weeks → forced structured review, not another silent reduction).
8. **Fueling modernized.** Working target ≥60 g carbohydrate/hour (trained toward 90 g/h with mixed glucose+fructose sources across Weeks 9–13); explicit carb-load (10–12 g/kg/day for 36–48 h) and race-morning meal (1–4 g/kg, finished 1–4 h before start).
9. **Contingency table and Week 12 go/no-go.** Written rules for missed days/weeks, and a scheduled decision where the race goal (time / completion / run-walk / defer) is chosen from evidence.
10. **Minimum viable dataset.** The capture system now degrades gracefully: two fields (post-run RPE, next-morning status) keep the control loop alive; everything else is optional enrichment. Capture completeness itself feeds the data-confidence flag.
11. **Confidence layer.** Farthest-ever runs are flagged as milestones, Week 13 is named the dress-rehearsal anchor, the weekly review now records *what went right*, and the race plan includes segmentation and a late-race mental plan.
12. **Time caps.** All long runs carry a time cap; distance targets are secondary to time on feet.

Heat note: training happens in Singapore, the race climate — chronic heat acclimation is built in. Keep long runs at race start time where practical so heat exposure matches race conditions; the acute heat-avoidance rules (§11.2) still apply on extreme days.

# How to use this document

1. Establish baselines and individualized intensity anchors before Week 1.
2. Capture Garmin data automatically and a minimal manual check-in before, after, and the next morning. If time is short, capture the minimum viable dataset: post-run RPE and next-morning status.
3. Classify the day green, amber, or red; apply the single action the status prescribes (§9).
4. Progress only one main lever per week: total volume, long-run duration, or quality exposure.
5. Escalate red flags and unresolved trends; never use dashboard reassurance to overrule symptoms.

**Medical boundary.** The plan organizes observations but cannot clear chest pain, fainting, unexplained breathlessness, neurologic symptoms, suspected heat illness, dark urine after exertion, infection, or gait-altering pain. Stop and seek assessment appropriate to severity.

**Wearable boundary.** VO2 max, training readiness, recovery time, HRV status, and race predictions are supporting signals. Trends under similar conditions matter more than single values; none overrides symptoms or clinical judgment.

# 1. Goal hierarchy and definition of success

| Level | What is optimized | How success is judged |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Safety and continuity | No unresolved red flags; issues do not progressively alter running or daily function. | Consistent weeks; minimal forced stoppage; timely review. |
| 2. Durability | Ability to absorb the long run and resume easy running in 24–48 h. | Next-morning response stable; gait normal; fatigue resolves. |
| 3. Aerobic development | More pace at the same effort, or less effort at the same pace. | Standardized field trend; HR/RPE relationship; drift. |
| 4. Marathon specificity | Fuel, hydration, equipment, pacing, and late-run mechanics remain reliable. | Rehearsals without material symptom or GI escalation. |
| 5. Performance | A realistic plan executed without early overreach. | Even effort, controlled first half, planned fueling, completion. |

No single metric is optimized in isolation. Weekly distance must be interpreted with intensity, long-run share, terrain, heat, sleep, fueling, strength load, training history, and symptom response.

**Under-loading is also a failure mode.** Arriving at race day chronically under-trained because the controller kept reducing is a plan failure, not a safety success. The minimum viable peak (§8) and the circuit-breaker (§9) exist to catch it.

# 2. Adaptive control loop

Plan — define purpose, duration/distance, intensity ceiling, route, heat strategy, fueling, and stop conditions.

Check — review symptoms, sleep, energy, illness, heat, and sensor confidence.

Execute — run by purpose and internal effort; in heat, slow pace to preserve intended intensity.

Reconcile — compare planned versus actual load, intensity, conditions, symptoms, fueling, and equipment.

Observe — capture immediate and next-morning response; delayed response is a key durability signal.

Adapt — proceed, hold, reduce, substitute, or request review. Change one progression lever at a time.

**Non-negotiable progression rule.** Do not deliberately increase both volume/duration and intensity in the same week. If quality is introduced or extended, hold volume and long run stable. If the long run grows, keep the rest easy.

## 3.1 Decision value of the metrics

- Weekly distance/time quantify exposure; they do not prove fitness or safety.
- Long-run duration is the early progression lever because the marathon demands time-on-feet durability — but it is capped relative to the week that supports it (share ≤ ~55%).
- Intensity distribution prevents unnoticed "moderate every day" drift.
- Session-RPE captures strain when heat, hills, sleep, or device error distort pace and HR.
- Pace-at-fixed-HR and aerobic drift require comparable route, conditions, and valid sensor data.
- Pain trend and next-morning function reveal whether tissue tolerance is keeping pace with cardiovascular fitness.
- Sleep, resting HR, and HRV are contextual trends, not single-day verdicts. Nightly HRV (lastNightAvg vs the personal baseline band) is used only as a 7-day rolling trend.
- Fueling, GI, skin, footwear, and heat are performance metrics because failure can end the race.

# 4. Data capture

## 4.1 Garmin automatic stream

- Elapsed/moving time, distance, route, elevation, cadence, lap splits, pace, and heart rate.
- Time in individualized HR zones and weather/temperature context where available. Use a chest strap when HR accuracy materially affects the decision.
- Resting HR, overnight HRV (nightly average and personal baseline band), sleep duration and score, and device wear time.
- VO2 max, acute load, recovery time, training status, and race prediction as secondary context only.
- Confidence flags for cadence lock, spikes/dropouts, loose optical sensor, missing overnight wear, GPS anomalies, and auto-pause distortion.

## 4.2 App capture moments

**Minimum viable dataset (the two fields that keep the control loop alive):** post-run RPE and next-morning issue status. If nothing else is captured, capture these. Everything below enriches the decision but is optional; capture completeness is itself tracked as a data-confidence input.

| Moment | Questions / fields | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Sleep quality; energy; mood; illness; general soreness; issue-specific next-morning status; confidence. | 30–45 s |
| Pre-run | Any red flag? Pain location/severity/character; gait effect; intention; heat/route; last meal/fuel. | 20–40 s |
| Post-run (core) | **RPE**; completed as intended? Pain trend; new symptom; GI/skin/foot; fuel g/h; fluid; equipment. | 45–60 s |
| Next morning after key run (core) | **Issue vs baseline**; stiffness duration (minutes); swelling; walking/stairs normal?; energy; ready for easy activity? | 30–45 s |
| Weekly review | Improved/worsened; recovery lag; adherence; confidence; plan vs actual; **what went right**; energy availability check; action and rationale. | 3–5 min |

## 4.3 Derived dashboard metrics

- Session load = RPE × minutes; trend weekly sum and key-session contribution.
- Long-run share = long-run distance ÷ weekly distance; **plan is designed to keep this ≤ ~55%; a planned week above 60% is a design error, not a training target.**
- Easy compliance = easy minutes inside the individualized ceiling ÷ planned easy minutes.
- Aerobic efficiency = pace/speed relative to HR on comparable route and environmental band.
- Decoupling = early versus late pace-to-HR relationship after excluding warm-up and terrain artifacts.
- Recovery lag = time until energy, soreness, issue status, and gait return to baseline, anchored to the next-morning check: *baseline by next morning / by 48 h / not by 48 h*.
- Issue burden = severity × persistence × functional impact, trended by location.
- Data confidence = sensor validity + capture completeness + condition comparability.

# 6. Intensity framework

Use individualized zones from a valid threshold assessment when available. Otherwise combine talk test, RPE, and observed HR. Pace is an output, especially in heat and humidity.

| Intensity | Practical anchor | Purpose | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Easy/recovery | RPE 2–3; full sentences; clearly below threshold; HR ceiling individualized | Aerobic volume, recovery, durability | ~80–85% of running time |
| Steady/marathon effort | RPE 3–4; controlled; short phrases; never forced | Specific rhythm, pacing calibration, fueling practice at race intensity | Default in scheduled blocks when green (§8); removed when amber |
| Threshold/controlled hard | RPE 6–7; sustainable intervals, not racing; individualized | Efficient aerobic-power maintenance | Default ≤1 short session/week when green; first thing removed when amber |
| Strides | 10–20 s relaxed-fast; full recovery; smooth | Neuromuscular economy | Default 4–8 reps 1–2×/week when symptom-free |
| Race pace | Derived from the Week 10 half-marathon result, course/heat, conservative execution | Race confidence and calibration | Progressive blocks Weeks 11–15 (§8) |

**Quality default rule.** Scheduled quality is the *default when green* and the *first casualty when amber*. Skipping quality on a green day is a plan deviation to record, same as skipping a run. The amber system is the flexibility mechanism; the plan does not hedge twice.

**Heat rule.** Preserve effort and accept slower pace. If faint, weak, confused, unusually dizzy, or showing heat-illness signs: stop, cool, and seek medical care appropriate to severity.

# 8. Detailed 16-week adaptive plan

Targets are envelopes, not entitlements. Week 16 training kilometres exclude the marathon. Weeks 1–5 default to four runs; from Week 6 a midweek medium-long run (MLR) makes five runs the default — it exists to spread load, not to pile on. Every long run carries a time cap; stop at the cap even if the distance target is unmet, and record the shortfall (do not make it up).

**Minimum viable peak (anti-under-loading guard).** By the end of Week 13, at least one fueled long run ≥26 km must be completed. If ambers have prevented it, the Week 12 go/no-go (below) must explicitly choose: attempt it in Week 13, adopt a run-walk completion strategy, or re-scope the goal. Silently arriving at the taper without it is not an option.

**Milestones.** Weeks 3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 11, and 13 each contain a farthest-ever run — flag and celebrate them. Week 10 is the tune-up race. Week 13 is the dress rehearsal: the confidence anchor for race day. "Finish with reserves" is the instruction for every one of them.

## 8.2 Contingency rules (missed training)

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Missed 1–3 days (logistics, minor illness) | Rejoin the plan where it is. Never make up missed distance. |
| Missed 4–7 days | Repeat the prior week's structure at ~90%, then continue. The race does not move; the peak does — drop the lowest-value week, not the absorb weeks. |
| Missed 2+ weeks (illness, injury, travel) | Drop back one phase, rebuild with one lever, and formally revisit the race goal at the next weekly review (completion / run-walk / defer). |
| Systemic illness (fever, chest symptoms) | No hard training. Return graded and clinician-led when significant. |
| Gates repeatedly failing before a key week | Do not decide on race morning. The Week 12 go/no-go (or an ad-hoc structured review) chooses the strategy explicitly. |

# 9. Green / amber / red adaptation rules

**Quantified wearable triggers.** A wearable signal becomes an amber *input* only when: the 7-day rolling HRV average sits outside the personal baseline band (Garmin's balanced range, e.g. 45–65 ms, refined by observation) — or resting HR sits ≥5 bpm above its 7-day baseline — **for ≥3 consecutive days**, *and* at least one symptom corroborates (fatigue, soreness, poor sleep, mood, illness signs). **Wearable-only deviations without symptoms default to green-easy: run the planned session at easy effort, no quality, and recheck tomorrow — do not reduce.** Single-day HRV/RHR readings are never acted on.

| Status | Signals | Action (one action, not a menu) |
|---|---|---|
| GREEN | No red flags; pain 0–2/10 and stable; normal gait; sleep/energy near baseline; no illness; expected recovery; adequate confidence. | Execute as planned, including scheduled quality. Progress only the designated lever. |
| AMBER — life | Two poor nights; corroborated HRV/RHR trend (rule above); high heat; travel; work stress; poor sensor confidence; minor illness uncertainty *without* focal symptoms. | Run easy, shorten ~20%, no quality. Recheck next morning. That is the whole action. |
| AMBER — tissue | Pain ~3/10 or rising but no gait change; unusual focal stiffness; next-morning function not back within 48 h; new swelling-free focal symptom. | Hold or reduce the *provoking* load 20–40%; convert quality to easy; substitute non-impact if impact is the problem. Recheck next morning; two consecutive tissue-ambers on the same site → issue review. |
| RED | Gait-changing/progressive pain; focal swelling; abnormal walk/hop; chest pain; fainting; severe breathlessness; confusion; severe dizziness; fever; suspected heat illness; dark urine; neurologic signs. | Stop/do not start. Seek urgent or timely clinical assessment. Wearable readiness cannot clear it. |

Pain numbers are not universal clearance thresholds. Character, location, trend, function, history, and clinician rules matter. A 2/10 focal pain that changes gait is red.

**Circuit-breaker.** Three amber days in any seven, or two consecutive weeks with reduced load, forces a structured review (self-review with the weekly template at minimum; coach/clinician where available) instead of another silent reduction. The review must answer: is this a load problem, a life problem, a fueling problem, or a medical problem — and what single change tests that hypothesis next week?

## 9.1 Weekly decision algorithm

1. Resolve safety first.
2. Distinguish logistics-related misses from symptom/recovery-related misses.
3. Review long-run response, issue burden, next-morning function, and recovery lag.
4. Review sleep, autonomic trend (rolling baselines only), illness, life stress, heat, and strength soreness.
5. Interpret aerobic signal only when conditions and data are comparable.
6. Choose one action: progress one lever, hold, reduce 10–30%, substitute, or review.
7. Record one-sentence rationale, **one thing that went right**, and what would change the decision next week.

| Decision | Use when | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Progress | Core gates green; recovery predictable; last progression absorbed. | Increase volume, long run, or quality — not more than one. |
| Hold | Mixed/uncertain signals, travel/heat, mild issue trend, new stressor. | Repeat key load; fix the uncertain variable. |
| Reduce | Recovery >48 h, accumulating fatigue, rising issue burden, repeated tissue-amber, illness recovery. | Cut 10–30%; remove quality first; shorten MLR before long run; shorten long run last. |
| Substitute | Impact is the problem but symptom-free aerobic work is appropriate. | Bike, elliptical, pool run, or walk easy; do not equate load automatically. |
| Clinical review | Gait change, focal worsening/swelling, suspected bone stress, recurrence, systemic or neurologic symptoms. | Pause provoking load; obtain appropriate review. |

# 10. Strength and tissue capacity

| Phase | Frequency | Emphasis | Guardrails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–8 | 2×/week, 30–45 min | Squat/lunge; hinge; straight/bent-knee calf; hip; hamstring; trunk; foot as indicated. | 2–4 sets; 2–3 reps in reserve; no failure. |
| Weeks 9–10 | 2×/week | Maintain strength; issue-specific capacity. | No novel DOMS before long run or the Week 10 race. |
| Weeks 11–14 | 1–2×/week | Maintain intensity; reduce sets/accessories. | Movement quality over volume. |

The plan's modest running volume is *justified by* the strength work — skipping strength B for three straight weeks converts this into a low-mileage plan with a large long run and nothing propping it up. Track strength completion as a first-class adherence metric.

**Achilles/heel boundary.** Tendon or heel loading must be individualized by the treating clinician. Track morning stiffness duration, loading pain, running pain, and next-day response separately.

# 11. Fueling, hydration, heat, and equipment

## 11.1 Fueling progression (gut training)

| Weeks | Long-run intake target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2–5 | 30–40 g carbohydrate/hour | Establish habit and tolerance on runs >75 min; record source, timing, water, GI response. |
| 6–8 | 40–50 g/h | Introduce the intended race product. |
| 9–10 | ≥50 g/h | Week 10 HM: practice race-frequency intake under race stress. |
| 11–13 | **≥60 g/h, trained toward 75–90 g/h** using multiple transportable carbohydrates (glucose+fructose) as tolerated | This is the race dose for a 4–5 h finisher; 30 g/h is a bonk-management dose, not a race dose. |
| 14–16 | Race dose, rehearsed and locked | Nothing new after Week 13. |

- Rehearse the pre-run meal under race-like conditions from Week 9.
- **Carb load:** 10–12 g/kg/day for the final 36–48 h before the race (Wednesday–Thursday of race week). **Race morning:** 1–4 g/kg, finished 1–4 h before the start — both rehearsed before long runs in Weeks 10 and 13.
- After demanding runs, prioritize normal food, carbohydrate replacement, protein, fluids, and sleep. Persistent inability to eat or rehydrate needs review.

## 11.2 Hydration and heat

- On selected long runs, use pre/post body mass plus fluid consumed to estimate sweat loss in that environment. Do not aim to gain weight while running.
- Individualize intake using thirst, access, sweat observations, weather, duration, and medical context; avoid both under-drinking and forced over-drinking.
- Singapore training doubles as race-climate acclimation. Schedule long runs at race start time where practical so heat exposure matches race conditions. On extreme days: run earlier/indoors, reduce pace/duration, use light clothing, shade, and cooling.
- Cramps, headache, nausea, dizziness, weakness, confusion, fainting, or low urine require stopping and assessment by severity. Dark urine plus muscle pain/weakness needs urgent review.

## 11.3 Equipment and skin

- Track shoes, approximate shoe distance, socks, insoles, lacing, clothing, lubricant/barrier, surface, and wet conditions.
- Treat chafing, blisters, toenails, and hot spots as constraints; fix recurrence before increasing long-run duration.
- Nothing new on race day: shoes, socks, clothing, fuel, carrying system, breakfast, and anti-chafe must be rehearsed.

## 11.4 Energy availability (proactive weekly check)

Under-fueling is the major upstream driver of bone stress injury during exactly the weeks (9–13) this plan loads hardest. The weekly review asks, every week: appetite normal? body-mass trend stable? (where relevant: menstrual function normal?) unusual cold intolerance, sleep disturbance, or motivation loss? Two consecutive concerning weeks → dietitian/medical review, before symptoms, not after.

# 12. Recovery and illness

| Domain | Track | Decision use |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep | Duration, quality, awakenings, disruption, 3–7 day trend | Two poor nights plus fatigue or corroborated biomarker change supports life-amber. |
| Autonomic trend | Resting HR and HRV vs personal rolling baseline; ≥3-day deviations only | Amber input only with symptom corroboration (§9); never diagnosis. |
| Soreness | General vs focal; severity; restriction; persistence | General soreness may modify; focal/function-changing gets issue review. |
| Energy/mood | Energy, motivation, irritability, unusual effort | Persistent decline suggests load, illness, under-fueling, stress, or medical issue. |
| Illness | Fever, chest/GI symptoms, cough, aches, medication | No hard training with systemic illness; return gradually/clinician-led when significant. |

**Recovery gate.** If a long run repeatedly leaves the runner substantially below baseline for more than 48 hours, the dose is too large or the supporting system — sleep, fuel, heat, health, or strength spacing — is inadequate.

# 13. Dashboard metrics and views

The first screen answers: "Proceed, hold, reduce, substitute, or review — and why?" It is not a wall of numbers.

| Layer | Primary display | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Today | Status; planned session; 2–4 reasons; stop/modify conditions; confidence badge | What to do now |
| Load | Weekly time/distance; long run + share; session-RPE; intensity split; strength completion | Whether exposure matches intent |
| Aerobic | Standardized HR–pace–RPE; drift; field test; environment band | Whether easy efficiency improves |
| Durability | Issue map; pain/function; next morning; recovery lag; gait/skin/foot | Whether tissues keep up |
| Recovery | Sleep; resting HR/HRV rolling baselines; energy/mood; illness; amber days | Whether to hold/reduce |
| Race systems | Fuel g/h trend vs target; fluid; GI; equipment; heat; rehearsal completeness | Whether the non-fitness plan is ready |
| Weekly decision | Plan vs actual; change; rationale; lever; what went right; clinician notes | What changes next week |

- Show trends and comparable-condition bands, not dramatic daily verdicts.
- Keep raw data behind the decision summary.
- Label device estimates and missing/low-confidence data.
- Never turn a green wearable score into clearance against symptoms — and never turn a wearable amber into a reduction without symptoms.
- Show planned-versus-actual and the reason; adherence alone is not success.
- Flag milestone runs (farthest-ever, tune-up, dress rehearsal) when they complete.
- Keep issue history location-specific and clinician-visible with consent; publish sensitive detail (pain maps, GI logs) only as aggregates.

# 14. Doctor and clinician review boundaries

| Boundary | Examples | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency/urgent | Chest pain/pressure; collapse/fainting; severe breathlessness; confusion; suspected heat stroke; seizure; dark urine with marked muscle pain/weakness; severe neurologic symptoms. | Stop; seek emergency or urgent care appropriate to the situation. |
| Prompt medical review | New exertional palpitations; recurrent dizziness; persistent fever/systemic illness; unexplained performance collapse; medication/heat concern; prolonged unexplained recovery. | Pause hard/long training and arrange review. |
| Sports medicine/physio/podiatry | Gait-changing pain; focal swelling; suspected bone stress; escalating Achilles/heel; recurrence across 2–3 sessions; walking/stairs affected. | Stop provoking load; obtain assessment and return-to-run guidance. |
| Dietitian/medical nutrition | Repeated GI failure; inability to meet needs; suspected or screened low energy availability (§11.4); unexplained weight change; restriction or deficiency concern. | Individualized assessment; labs only when clinically indicated. |
| Plan-managed | Brief stable soreness; isolated sensor anomaly; one poor night; early skin hotspot; logistics miss. | Modify locally; capture outcome; escalate if persistent/worse. |

A doctor may set stricter boundaries based on age, history, medication, examination, tests, family history, or known conditions. Individualized instructions supersede this plan.

# 15. Race week and execution

- Confirm health, logistics, weather, start time, transport, sleep, breakfast, gear, anti-chafe, fuel, fluid access, and emergency contacts.
- Carb load Wednesday–Thursday (10–12 g/kg/day); race-morning meal 1–4 g/kg finished 1–4 h before start — both already rehearsed.
- Run the opening by conservative effort calibrated from the Week 10 half and the Week 11–14 MP blocks — not adrenaline or watch prediction; adjust for heat/course.
- Use rehearsed fuel from early in the race at the trained rate (≥60 g/h); do not wait for depletion or try untested products.
- Use lap-average pace/effort and body signals; ignore transient GPS errors.
- **Segment the race:** 0–10 km settle (deliberately conservative, fuel from 20–30 min); 10–25 km rhythm (locked MP effort, fuel on schedule); 25–35 km work (the part rehearsed in Weeks 11–13 — expect it to feel like the dress rehearsal's final blocks); 35–42 km execute (shorter mental chunks: next aid station, next km; use the prepared phrases/mantras from training).
- **If the goal slips mid-race:** drop one level in the goal hierarchy and execute that level well — pre-deciding this is part of the Week 12 go/no-go.
- If symptoms cross a red boundary, stop and seek assistance; completion is subordinate to safety.

**Race goal hierarchy.** A-goal: healthy start and controlled completion. B-goal: execute pacing, fueling, and heat strategy. C-goal: the time target chosen at the Week 12 go/no-go, only if the Week 10 half and MP-block evidence support it.

# 16. Post-race recovery and evaluation

| Window | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–24 h | Cool safely, rehydrate, eat tolerated food, inspect feet/skin, monitor heat/rhabdo signs, avoid driving if unwell. |
| 24–72 h | Walk/mobilize as tolerated; no forced recovery run; record pain, swelling, sleep, appetite, urine, and function. |
| Days 4–14 | Return gradually after normal gait, resolving soreness, and energy; delay if focal symptoms persist. |
| Review | Compare pacing, HR/RPE, fuel/fluid, GI, equipment, symptoms, and recovery; document what the system got right/wrong. |

# 17. Weekly review template

- Outcome — planned/completed km and minutes; long run; key session; strength sessions completed.
- Intent — easy compliance; quality completed/modified (on green days, skipped quality is a deviation); weekly session-RPE.
- Durability — issues; worst severity; gait/function; next-morning response; recovery lag.
- Recovery — sleep; resting HR/HRV rolling-baseline context; energy/mood; illness/stress; strength soreness.
- Energy availability — appetite; body-mass trend; (where relevant) menstrual function; cold intolerance/sleep/motivation flags.
- Environment/systems — heat; fuel g/h vs this week's target; fluid; GI; skin/foot/equipment.
- Data confidence — high/medium/low and why (including capture completeness).
- **What went right this week** — ____.
- Decision — progress/hold/reduce/substitute/review.
- One lever and rationale — ____.
- Stop/modify conditions next week — ____.

# 18. Interpretation cautions

- There is no universally safe weekly percentage increase. History, tolerance, intensity, terrain, frequency, recovery, and symptoms all matter.
- Lower HR at a pace can mean fitness, but also weather, fatigue, sensor error, hydration, medication, or altered effort.
- HRV/resting HR are person-specific; use rolling baselines and symptoms, not comparisons to others — and never single days.
- Pain needs location, quality, onset, during-run trend, next-day response, and functional effect — not only a number.
- Lab tests are snapshots; repeatable field data and real-world durability drive weekly adaptation.
- The peak is deliberately modest relative to some conventional plans: continuity and tolerance are prioritized over mileage. The controller guards against over-loading *and* under-loading; both are failures.
