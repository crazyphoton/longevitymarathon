The Plan / Versions / v4

Plan v4 — current

17 weeks, effective 19 Aug 2026 (mid-Week 2), race day Fri 4 Dec in Week 17. v4 keeps v3's adaptive control system — the gates, time caps, and fueling ladder — and replaces its volume schedule with Dr. Varun Reddy's ramp: 15% per week from 20 km to a 50 km peak held for two weeks, then down to race day.

Current Effective from 19 Aug 2026· Created by: Nishanth Sudharsanam· Prescribed by: Dr. Varun Reddy, 19 Aug 2026 review

Summary: rebuild from 20 km/week at 15% per week — 20, 23, 26, 30, 35, 40, 46 — to a 50 km peak held Weeks 9–10, then a descent through the half-marathon tune-up (Week 11), the go/no-go (Week 13), and a reduced-volume dress rehearsal (Week 14) to race day. The fueled ≥26 km long run — the minimum viable peak — lands in the Week 9–10 peak, the only weeks where it fits the ≤55% long-run share rule. No strides while the heel issue is active. Strength narrows to hip, core, calf, and knee — with drop heel raises, in Dr. Reddy's words, “specifically a loading stretch” — plus a daily stretching protocol. Gates, time caps, and gut training (≥60 g carbohydrate/hour) carry over from v3.

Change reason: Dr. Varun Reddy's 19 Aug review, made independently of the AI critiques: two idle weeks followed by ~40 km in seven days was too sharp a ramp even at easy pace, and biomechanical risk outranks cardiovascular risk. Garmin history back to mid-June confirmed the base v3 assumed never existed. Full reasoning in the change history and the review entry.

The adaptive rules, in brief

Every day is classified green, amber, or red, and the status prescribes one action:

  • Green — execute as planned, including scheduled quality. Skipping quality on a green day is a recorded deviation.
  • Amber (life) — two poor nights, a corroborated HRV/resting-HR trend (7-day rolling average off the personal baseline for 3+ consecutive days plus a symptom), heat, travel, stress: run easy, shorten ~20%, no quality. That is the whole action.
  • Amber (tissue) — pain ~3/10 or rising, unusual focal stiffness, next-morning function not back within 48 h: reduce the provoking load 20–40% and recheck next morning.
  • Red — symptoms only (chest pain, fainting, gait-changing pain, dark urine, heat illness…): stop. No wearable score can clear a red, and no wearable score can create one.

Wearable-only deviations without symptoms default to an easy run, not a reduction. Three amber days in seven forces a structured review. A minimum viable peak — at least one fueled 26 km+ long run by end of Week 13, expected in the Week 9–10 peak — guards against the quieter failure mode: arriving at race day under-trained because the controller kept saying “careful”.

Missed training has written rules too: 1–3 days — rejoin, never make up distance; 4–7 days — repeat the prior week at ~90%; 2+ weeks — drop back a phase and formally revisit the race goal.

The full framework — metric hierarchy, capture schema, fueling and heat protocols, clinician boundaries, and references — lives in the plan document Adaptive_Marathon_Plan_v3_17_Week.md (v4 ramp recorded as a dated section and doctor's comment) in the project repository; the operational data (day-level sessions, gates, weekly decisions) lives in the project database and drives the dashboard.
