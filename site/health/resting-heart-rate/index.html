Resting heart rate

Heart rate at rest, typically measured first thing in the morning or overnight during sleep.

Why it may matter here

A relatively accessible, low-burden signal of cardiovascular fitness and recovery status — and one of the more common ways training adaptation is discussed outside a lab.

How to read it
Meaningful as a multi-week trend under consistent conditions. Single-day spikes usually reflect poor sleep, heat, alcohol, or stress — not a change in fitness.
Read the measurement methodology

Source & method

Garmin, nightly, read as a rolling weekly average.

Measurement caveats

Night-to-night RHR is noisy: alcohol, poor sleep, illness, heat, and travel all move it independently of fitness. Single-night readings are not reported as meaningful on their own; the site prefers a rolling average and flags unusual single nights rather than treating them as trend.
