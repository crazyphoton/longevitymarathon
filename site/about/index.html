About this project

Who's running this, why, and what the site will and won't claim on their behalf.

The author

I'm the person doing the training and writing the entries. I'm not a professional athlete, a coach, or an exercise scientist — I've run before, inconsistently, and never trained for anything this structured. That's part of the point: the project is meant to show what a plan built by someone with no real expertise looks like when it meets expert review and real life, not to showcase an already-optimized athlete.

A full public byline, portrait, and named biography are still pending final confirmation before launch. Until then, this site is written consistently in the first person rather than filled in with a placeholder name.

Dr. Varun Reddy

Dr. Varun Reddy is a collaborator on this project: a clinician-scientist with a surgical and research background, including early work in cognitive neuroscience and regenerative medicine, now focused on preventive, precision-oriented healthspan medicine. He was previously the founding Chief Medical Officer of Elyx, a longevity-focused concierge clinic in Singapore — a role that has since ended. His review of the training plan is in progress; see his profile page for the full background and how his commentary will be attributed.

The project boundary

This site documents one person's four-month marathon preparation and whatever happens to his health markers along the way. It's an experiment in public, not a research study, not a coaching program, and not medical advice. Health findings are reported regardless of direction — improvement, decline, or nothing notable at all — and a single person's results are never presented as proof of a general health claim. The full reasoning behind that boundary lives on The Experiment.

Editorial principles, briefly

  • Health markers are observed, not scored as wins or losses.
  • The plan, its revisions, and actual training stay distinguishable — nothing gets silently rewritten.
  • Estimates, home readings, lab measurements, and subjective reports are never presented as if they were the same kind of number.
  • Mistakes, missed sessions, and bad decisions are published, not edited out.
