# Longevity Marathon — Voice & Editorial QC Guide

**Version:** 2.0 (condensed)  
**Purpose:** A sparse voice/rhetoric checklist for reviewing copy on the Longevity Marathon website.  
**Scope:** This document owns voice, tone, and prose mechanics only — not page content, structure, or approved copy. For information architecture, page requirements, and canonical copy (including the Home hero and the disagreement-framework wording), see `longevity_marathon_website_spec_v2.md`. For visual/design language, see `design.md`. This is used mainly by QC/review agents checking whether copy sounds right — the main build is driven by the website spec.

---

## 1. Editorial north star

Longevity Marathon is a person documenting a serious experiment in public. The writing should sound like a thoughtful, observant person working through a real question — rigorous enough to earn trust, accessible enough to read without specialist knowledge, human enough to contain uncertainty, emotion, awkward details, revisions, and occasional dry humour.

Not a research paper. Not a lifestyle brand, motivational campaign, or sales funnel. Authority comes from specificity, transparent reasoning, and a willingness to show what didn't go to plan.

> **Cut generic filler, not warmth or rhythm.**

Every page should: say something real (not generic health-site language); let the reader follow the thinking; respect uncertainty (separate what happened from what might explain it); remain readable to a smart non-expert; keep the person visible (sore legs, doubt, relief, irritation — not just data).

Voice: **a smart friend documenting a serious experiment.** First person is fine; admitting uncertainty and revising an assumption is fine; the prose should not pose as detached scholarship.

## 2. Voice and tone

**Serious and rigorous, without sounding academic.** Express care about facts and uncertainty through clear prose, not research-paper mannerisms.

> Prefer: "My resting heart rate fell during the first six weeks. Training may be part of the reason, but the period also included better sleep and less travel."
> Avoid: "The observed reduction in resting heart rate cannot be conclusively attributed to the intervention due to multiple confounding variables."

**Conversational, warm, human.** Personality, emotional reactions, mild humour, and small moments from daily life are welcome. Warmth comes from observation and candour, not forced friendliness. Don't attach a tidy moral to every paragraph — a moment can stay unresolved.

**Meta and self-aware writing is allowed** when it reveals something real about the work or the thinking. The problem is *frivolous framing* — inventing a false assumption just to create a neat reversal.

**Emotional texture without melodrama.** Name emotions in proportion to the event.

> Prefer: "By kilometre 14, the pace had slipped again. I was annoyed because the first half had felt controlled."
> Avoid: "At kilometre 14, I entered the crucible where doubt and determination collided."

**Sentence rhythm.** Mix short, medium, and long sentences. Use a short sentence for weight, not as a default. Let a paragraph develop before breaking it apart. Read aloud — if it sounds clipped or like a sequence of captions, restore connective tissue. Concision means removing waste, not removing warmth.

## 3. Earned contrast

Constructions like "not X, but Y," "this isn't just X," "more than X," "rather than X" imply a reader belief. They're valid only when that belief is real and relevant.

Before approving: What exactly is X? Would a reasonable reader actually assume X? Has the page established X as a live possibility? If not — cut the contrast, state Y directly.

> Frivolous: "I'll publish the runs, tests, and setbacks—not to prove a point, but to see what changes." (Nothing established that readers assumed this was about proving a point.)
> Earned: "My original goal was to hold the planned pace at any cost. After the calf pain returned, the goal changed: finish the training block without turning a warning sign into an injury." (The earlier position was established; the change is real.)

## 4. Functional repetition

Repetition is fine when it does a job: rhythm, emphasis, orientation, safety, reinforcement, or showing change over time. For every repeated idea, ask: **what new work does the second mention do?** If none — cut or combine.

> Empty: "I'm training for a marathon in four months. This four-month marathon experiment tracks how training affects healthspan. Over four months, I'll document the marathon journey and its healthspan effects." (Same premise three times, no development.)
> Functional: "Four months is not much time to prepare for a marathon. It is long enough, though, for the plan to affect sleep, appetite, blood markers, work, and the parts of my legs that complain first." ("Four months" returns with new consequences.)

## 5. Evidence and causality language

This is about phrasing only — the underlying evidence/medical *policy* (attribution, safety boundaries, what can be published) lives in the website spec (§9.2–9.3, §12.5).

- "After" does not mean "because of." Prefer "coincided with," "may have contributed to," "is consistent with," or a plain account of sequence.
- Don't imply one person's result proves a general health effect.
- State a meaningful alternative explanation without a ritual list of caveats.
- Let the source of a claim be clear in ordinary prose (feeling vs. measurement vs. device estimate vs. clinician view vs. research) without labelling every sentence.

> "Garmin estimated my VO2 max at 48. That is a useful trend to watch, not a laboratory measurement."

## 6. Anti-AI-slop scan

Reject unless quoted, discussed critically, or used in an unusually concrete and justified way: *embark on/upon, unlock your potential, transformative journey, tapestry, treasure trove, game-changer, life-changing, beast mode, biohack(ing) as promotional identity, revolutionise/revolutionary (no literal revolution), elevate your, supercharge, unleash, the ultimate guide, a testament to, ever-evolving landscape, at the intersection of, in today's fast-paced world, delve into, holistic journey, data-driven optimisation as image rather than description.*

Flag for contextual review: "This isn't just X; it's Y." / "It's not about X. It's about Y." / "More than X, Y." / "From X to Y" with no real progression / "Whether you're A, B, or C..." / "Here's the thing..." as filler / "The truth is..." before an unsupported opinion / "Ultimately," + generic summary / "In a world where..." / "Imagine..." as an unearned opening / serial rhetorical questions / repeated "clear, simple, powerful" adjective triads / three consecutive sections with the same sentence shape.

Also flag: unsupported superlatives and causal verbs (*best, proven, transformed, caused, fixed, prevented, reversed*); a paragraph restating its opening sentence without adding anything; passages that could appear unchanged on any other fitness/wellness site.

Headlines should feel inviting and chapter-like — a real question, moment, decision, or finding — not salesy hero copy. The approved Home headline and other canonical copy live in the website spec §18; don't invent competing headlines here.

## 7. Review output

**Verdict:** PASS / REVISE / REJECT.

**Severity:**
- **S0** — safety/factual: unqualified medical advice, unsupported diagnosis, false fact, causal health claim presented as established. → normally REJECT until fixed.
- **S1** — trust/meaning: misrepresents what was measured or who interpreted it, hides a material limitation, exaggerates a finding, misattributes advice. → REVISE or REJECT.
- **S2** — voice/structure/clarity: generic AI-like passage, frivolous contrast, empty repetition, salesy headline, over-compressed prose, weak focus. → normally REVISE.
- **S3** — polish: minor rhythm, small redundancy, word choice. → may accompany PASS or REVISE.

**Scores (1–5 each):**
- purpose — does the page do its job? (see the page's Purpose in website spec §6)
- specificity — real observations/numbers/decisions, not generic filler (check against the page's actual content in website spec §6–§8)
- trust — evidence, uncertainty, attribution handled honestly (see §5 above; policy in website spec §9.2–9.3, §12.5)
- voice, rhythm, restraint, contrast, repetition — see §§2–4, 6 above
- accessibility — followable by a smart non-expert (website spec §4.3, progressive disclosure)
- page fit — suits the specific page's charts/links/interface elements (website spec §6.x for that page)

Any S0 overrides the score; PASS normally needs an average ≥4 with nothing below 3.

For each issue: severity, quoted excerpt/location, diagnosis, why it matters, concrete rewrite. For each flagged contrast: name the assumed belief, mark established/reasonable/invented. For each flagged repetition: mark KEEP / COMBINE / CUT.

## 8. Compact system prompt for the editor agent

```text
You are the voice/copy QC reviewer for Longevity Marathon, a public four-month marathon-training experiment. You check tone and prose mechanics only — page content, structure, and approved copy are defined in the website spec; don't relitigate those.

Edit toward a voice that is serious and rigorous but conversational, warm, grounded, and human — a thoughtful person documenting a real experiment, not a research paper, lifestyle brand, or generic AI explainer. Preserve emotional texture, uncertainty, and breathing room; mix sentence lengths; cut generic filler, not warmth or rhythm.

Apply the earned-contrast rule to every "not X, but Y" or similar construction: name the implied X, cut the contrast if no reasonable reader would assume it.

Apply the functional-repetition rule: each repeated idea must do identifiable new work, or cut/combine it.

Flag banned and watch-list phrases, unsupported superlatives/causal verbs, and passages that could appear on any other wellness site. Check causality phrasing (coincided with / may have contributed to, not "after" implying "because").

Return: (1) PASS/REVISE/REJECT; (2) one-sentence assessment; (3) 1–5 scores across purpose, specificity, trust, voice, rhythm, restraint, contrast, repetition, accessibility, page fit; (4) issues ordered S0→S3; (5) earned-contrast audit; (6) repetition audit (KEEP/COMBINE/CUT); (7) concrete rewrites; (8) what to preserve.
```

## Closing calibration

Longevity Marathon should not sound immaculate. It should sound considered — curious, amused, disappointed, uncertain, or pleased, as long as those reactions arise from something that actually happened.

**Cut generic filler, not warmth or rhythm.**
